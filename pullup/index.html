Why it's hard

It's a strength-to-weight thing.

A pull-up is you lifting your whole body with your back and arms. So it comes down to two things: how strong you are, and how much of you there is to lift. That's why plenty of big, strong blokes can't do one either.

The 1% myth

“Only 1% of women can do a pull-up.”

You've probably seen this one going around. Nobody measured it. There's no decent study on how many adults can do a pull-up, men or women. Here's what we actually have:

4 of 17
women who couldn't do a pull-up got their first one after 3 months of training three times a week (University of Dayton).
55%
of female US Marine recruits couldn't do 3 pull-ups at the end of boot camp in 2013.

So yes, it's hard. The lesson isn't “you can't”. It's give it months, not weeks.

Get stronger.

That's what the rest of this guide is about.

Get lighter.

Not required, and plenty of people get there without it. But if you're losing fat anyway, every kilo off is a kilo you don't have to pull.

The self-check

Four tests. Five minutes.

Do them in this order with 2 to 3 minutes' rest between each. Burning muscles are fine. Sharp pain means stop.

The three stages

Where you are decides what you train.

Stage 1

Build the base

For you if
You can't hang for 30 seconds yet, or inverted rows feel hard.
Train
Dead hangs, scapular pulls, inverted rows and lat pulldowns.
Move on when
You can hang for 30 seconds and do 8 inverted rows with the bar at hip height.

Stage 2

First rep

For you if
Your grip and back are there. You just can't get your chin over the bar yet.
Train
Slow lowers, top holds, band-assisted pull-ups and heavier pulldowns.
Move on when
Chin over the bar from a dead hang. One clean rep.

Stage 3

One to five

For you if
You can do one, maybe a few.
Train
Lots of single reps, a slow lower straight after, plus rows and pulldowns.
Done when
3 sets of 5 with a rep left in the tank.

What slows people down

Five mistakes I see all the time.

  1. Living on the band

    Bands help most at the bottom and least at the top, which is exactly where most people get stuck. Use them, just not on their own.

  2. Only doing lat pulldowns

    They help, but sitting under a cable isn't the same as moving your whole body. Keep them and add the hanging work.

  3. Testing every session

    Maxing out every time mostly makes you tired. Train it twice a week and test every four.

  4. Half reps

    Arms straight at the bottom, chin over at the top. Kipping and half reps feel like progress. They aren't.

  5. Training it once a week

    Twice a week is the minimum. Three is fine if your elbows and shoulders are happy.

How long it takes

6 weeks to 6 months

That's the honest answer. It depends on where you start, how often you train it, how you sleep, and whether your weight's going up or down. Anyone promising you a pull-up in 30 days doesn't know where you're starting from.

Redo the self-check every 4 weeks. If the numbers are going up, it's working, even before the first rep turns up.

Questions

Things people ask me.

Pull-up or chin-up?

Pull-up is palms facing away, chin-up is palms facing you. Chin-ups are usually a bit easier because your biceps help more. Either is a great first goal. Just test and train the same grip so your numbers mean something.

Do I need to lose weight first?

No. Plenty of people get their first pull-up without losing a kilo. It just comes quicker if your weight's heading down rather than up.

Is it harder for women?

On average, yes. Women tend to carry less upper-body muscle for their body weight. But an average doesn't decide whether you get one, and the training is exactly the same.

Can I do this at home?

Yes, with a doorway bar and a resistance band. Swap inverted rows for band rows and pulldowns for band pulldowns. Make sure the bar is rated for your weight.

My elbows or shoulders hurt. Should I push through?

No. Drop the exercises that hurt, keep the ones that don't, and see a physio if it hangs around for more than a week or two.
